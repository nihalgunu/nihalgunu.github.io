I build AI systems for places where you can't afford to be wrong. Right now that means robot learning research at Purdue and enterprise AI at Phyvant.

I like building infrastructure that works under constraints. Private, accurate, and fast, when conventional wisdom says pick two.

Long-term I want to work on general intelligence for robots: one policy that transfers across embodiments and tasks instead of a new model for every arm and every skill. The part I keep coming back to is whether we can actually tell when one of these is getting better. Most reported improvements in this field are smaller than the noise around them, which is what my current research is about.

interests

Generalist robot policies: vision-language-action models, flow-matching and diffusion action heads, cross-embodiment transfer. How you evaluate them honestly, which is harder than it looks and is where most of my time goes. Sim-to-real, and the distance between a benchmark number and a robot that works in a room nobody cleaned first. Human-aware planning and control under uncertainty.

Also: RLHF (PPO, DPO). Data curation for specialized models, less data and higher signal. Privacy-preserving ML. Distributed systems that don't go down.

I also think a lot about the business side of enterprise software. Longer cycles, more stakeholders, higher trust requirements. Frustrating and fascinating.
