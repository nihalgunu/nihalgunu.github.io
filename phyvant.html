2025

Every company now buys the same models from the same labs at the same price. Whatever the frontier learns this quarter, it learns for your competitor the same morning. The model is what everyone shares. It cannot be what sets you apart.

What remains yours is judgment: which deductions to dispute, how a contract term applies to this quarter's money, what a determination owes. A few experts hold it. They have each made the call thousands of times, and not one of them can write down how. It is the last input a competitor cannot buy, and it is stored in people who are leaving.

This is an older problem than software. Every craft ran on skill locked in masters until the drawing and the jig converted that skill into equipment the shop owned instead of a person it rented. That conversion made scale possible. It has not happened for judgment work.

The industry's two answers fail the same test. The first is to ask: consultants, interviews, documentation sprints. Asking collects what people can say, and the rules that matter are the part they cannot. You cannot ask for what cannot be said. The second is to push your data into weights, which feels like ownership and is not: a fact welded into a model cannot be read, audited, or corrected. Both roads rent you someone else's intelligence.

Ours is a different bet. The judgment got written down anyway, in the work. A disputed deduction is not a judgment call in the abstract; it is the hundred your team already settled and the terms they leaned on each time. Every resolved case is a worked example of a rule nobody could state. You see it in crowd navigation: people follow consistent rules not one of them can articulate, and you recover them from recorded behavior or not at all. A back office is the same system with money attached.

So Phyvant reads the finished work and recovers the procedure your team was already running. What comes out is not a model that absorbed your company. It is a procedure you can read, argue with, and version, and it runs inside your own cloud.

The companies that win will be the ones that turn the judgment sitting in their experts' heads into outcomes they own.

We are ex-Mercor, Stanford, and NASA. We have built RL environments and identified exoplanets. Phyvant runs today inside America's largest private company, where a wrong number is an audit finding. The problems we are working on:

  • Reading judgment out of finished work instead of out of interviews
  • Proving a system against an expert, case by case, before it is allowed to act
  • Execution that returns the same answer on every rerun
  • Knowing which cases the system has not earned, and routing those to a person

Every craft got its jig in the end. This is judgment's.
